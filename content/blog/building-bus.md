Geri always has a goal in mind. When I got to know him at the age of 16 he already finished a successful car racer career and just started his new job at the local mall's bakery. Shortly, in a couple of years period he also tried various roles such as courier, warehouse worker and print operator of a sportswear company. He never stopped tinkering. From flat-renovation to buggy car building (yes, a real car from scratch!) he was always busy with something. So I was not very surprised when he announced one day that he bought a crappy tourist bus for 3000 EURs. The plan was to convert it to a camper. So what's the big deal here?

On a chilly Sunday afternoon, when I was helping out with some minor tasks around the bus, Geri suddenly asked for my honest opinion:
> *"Do you think this whole thing is actually worth it?"*

He was seemingly in a low point as the repair took longer and it ate up more money than he expected, so real hesitation appeared on his face. But this couldn't have been unexpected, as a bunch of his former projects ended up the same way: a lot of suffering and financial loss. For instance the overall cost of the buggy was around double what he eventually sold it for. So the main difference with those cases is that he never really cared. It was never about the money. Never about impressing anyone. Just pure joy and exploration. Something has changed, but what?

Half an-hour ago he gave me a tour inside the Setra, showing where the bed will be and how will the space problem be solved (spoiler: swivel stool). If you ever met with an enterpreneur, artist, child or any visioner you know this phase. They already see the final goal and the imperfect reality just fades away. Usually at this point they simply grab a hammer and bend the reality to match the dream. Okay, so he still believes in the future, yet there are doubts.

> *"Some say it's just a waste of time and money. It's a pile of rust."*

And then it clicks. We are no longer teenagers who can just freely mess around. Time gets more precious as you get older since you have less of it. And while you are elbow-deep in motor-oil, others are climing higher and higher up some corporate ladder, cruising foreign roads in their company cars.

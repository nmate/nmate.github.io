Geri is a good old friend of mine. Since I know him, he has been always tinkering with something. Just to name a few of the wildest projects: building a buggy car from scratch, restoring rusty old motorbikes or buying and renovating a flat in a risky neighborhood for making some profit. He is the free-soul type of guy who does not care much about the 'what-ifs', and just takes the move if a tempting opportunity appears on the horizon. This is how he managed to travel and work all around Europe from the UK to Austria, without even having a language exam. He just does not need that. Being street-smart with a good sense of humour solves everything.

So I wasn't much surprised, when he announced one day that **he bought a crappy Setra tourist bus for 3000 EURs**. Although he was planning to build a camp van before, I also knew that he likes challenges - which explained the huge difference in size to a van. The plan was to convert the bus into a static camper. So what's the big deal here?

On a chilly Sunday afternoon, when I was helping out with the bus, he asked me something that he never asked before:
> *"Do you think if this bus project makes any sense?"*

I had mixed feelings hearing this. Obviously the naysayers had punched a hole in his armour, no matter how sure he was of himself and his goal. *"It will never pay off"*, *"You'll never be able to sell it"*, *"Won't be good for anything"* - the sort of things you hear from quite a few people.

So I was wondering if there's any logical reason that could explain why someone sinks a load of money and free hours into a heap of rust? **And interestingly, just by removing the economical benefits from the top priority, I have found no reason why such an experiment would be a bad idea.** Taking Geri's example he gained the following skills during this project:
- alma
- korte
- banan


...

Just half-an-hour before he gave me a tour inside the Setra, showing where the bed will be and how he will make space in the "living room". If you ever met with a visioner who believes in what he or she is doing, you know this phenomena. The imperfect reality fades away and you also start to see with the eyes of the master. Then these people just grab a hammer and bend the reality to match their dreams.



He never asked me anything like that before. I knew that the repair took longer and it ate up more money than he expected, so I kind of understood the hesitation on his face. But it wasn't so unexpected at the same time. A bunch of his former projects ended up the same way: a lot of suffering and financial loss. For instance the overall cost of the buggy was way higher than he eventually sold it for. But he never really cared, it wasn't about the money. Never about impressing anyone. Just pure joy and exploration.

So before saying anything, I tried to figure out what has changed?

Because half-an-hour ago he gave me a tour inside the Setra, showing where the bed will be and how the space problem will be solved in the "living room" (spoiler: with swivel stool). If you ever met with an enterpreneur, artist, child or any visioner you know this phase. They already see the final goal and the imperfect reality just fades away. Usually at this point they simply grab a hammer and bend the reality to match the dream.

> *"Some say it's just a waste of time and money. It's a pile of rust."*

And then it clicked. We are no longer teenagers who can just freely mess around. Time gets more precious as we get older since we have less of it. And while you are elbow-deep in motor-oil, others are climbing higher and higher up some corporate ladder, cruising foreign roads in their company cars.


...


Geri always has a goal in mind. When I got to know him at the age of 16 he already finished a successful car racer career and just started his new job at the local mall's bakery. Shortly, in a couple of years period he also tried various roles such as courier, warehouse worker and print operator of a sportswear company. He never stopped tinkering. From flat-renovation to buggy car building (yes, a real car from scratch!) he was always busy with something. So I was not very surprised when he announced one day that he bought a crappy tourist bus for 3000 EURs. The plan was to convert it to a camper. So what's the big deal here?
